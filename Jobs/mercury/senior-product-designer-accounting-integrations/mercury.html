I'm not a designer learning accounting for this role — I run a private tax practice, TaxesByRoss (150+ clients since 2015), and I've built my own software to manage all my clients' affairs and other tools, including an application called ReconcileIQ for reconciliation.

I also built the pipeline that carries a client's documents all the way to the books. That pipeline is close to Mercury's own problem statement: people bouncing between banks, spreadsheets, and receipts just to prepare transactions for their books. I live in that friction every quarter, for 150+ clients.

I customized an open-source invoice extractor (Well, by WellApp.ai) into that pipeline — the part I built is the layer between extraction and a filed return: a bridge that maps every line item to a Schedule C category, assigns the quarter from the transaction date, and writes it into the books, additive only, never overwriting a hand-entered row. For nonprofit clients, the same pipeline auto-fills a Form 990 from the general ledger.

I also built InsureBridge, a life-insurance enrollment platform spanning three portals — consumer, agent, and admin — off one shared data model, so a change in one view never disagrees with another.

Most recently I designed and built the platform behind TOBA, a 47-year-old Tampa nonprofit with 500+ members that had no way to collect dues at all before this — working with its board as product stakeholders, delivering a written handoff, and resolving three real board objections — data sovereignty, duplication, cost — to a shipped result.
